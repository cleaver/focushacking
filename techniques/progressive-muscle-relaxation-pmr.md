## Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)

Edmund Jacobson's technique of systematically tensing and releasing each major muscle group in sequence. By producing a deep physical relaxation state, PMR reduces the bodily tension and cortisol that impair prefrontal cortex function and sustained attention.

### Example

Olympic athletes, test pilots, and surgeons use PMR before high-stakes performances. Some elite chess players (including world champions) use abbreviated versions before tournament games to clear physical tension that interferes with analytical thinking.

## Papers

### 2024

1. **Efficacy of Progressive Muscle Relaxation in Adults for Stress, Anxiety, and Depression: A Systematic Review** — *Systematic review*
   - Authors: Khir et al.
   - N=3402
   - Effect: Large increase
   - PMR is effective in reducing stress, anxiety, and depression in adults across 46 publications from 16 countries; combined PMR with other interventions showed enhanced efficacy.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.2147/PRBM.S437277)

2. **Effectiveness of nurse-led progressive muscle relaxation on stress, anxiety, and depression: A randomized controlled trial** — *RCT*
   - Authors: George et al.
   - N=61
   - Effect: Large increase
   - Ten sessions of Jacobson PMR produced statistically significant reductions in stress and anxiety after 5 sessions and in depression, anxiety, and stress after 10 sessions compared to treatment-as-usual control.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.4103/jehp.jehp_1913_23)

### 2021

1. **Effectiveness of Progressive Muscle Relaxation, Deep Breathing, and Guided Imagery in Promoting Psychological and Physiological States of Relaxation** — *RCT*
   - Authors: Toussaint et al.
   - N=60
   - Effect: Large increase
   - PMR significantly increased both psychological and physiological relaxation vs. control, with significant effects on electrodermal activity and heart rate as objective markers of reduced sympathetic nervous system arousal.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/5924040)

### 2008

1. **Relaxation training for anxiety: a ten-years systematic review with meta-analysis** — *Meta-analysis*
   - Authors: Manzoni et al.
   - Effect: Large increase
   - Relaxation training including Jacobson's progressive relaxation demonstrated significant anxiety reduction across a decade of studies (1997–2007), with consistent effects across clinical and non-clinical populations.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-244X-8-41)
