  2. Close your eyes and direct attention to the physical sensation of breathing — the rise of the chest, air at the nostrils.

## Mindfulness Meditation

A breath-anchored attention practice where you observe thoughts, sensations, and sounds without judgment, repeatedly redirecting wandering attention back to the breath. Regular practice strengthens the neural circuitry of sustained focus.

### Example

A surgeon meditates for 15 minutes before complex procedures, reporting enhanced intraoperative focus and reduced anxiety. Emergency medicine physicians use brief sessions between cases to reset attention.

## Papers

### 2023

1. **Mindfulness enhances cognitive functioning: a meta-analysis of 111 randomized controlled trials** — *Meta-analysis*
   - Authors: Zainal N. H. & Newman M. G.
   - N=9538
   - Effect: Moderate increase
   - MBIs had small-to-moderate significant effects on global cognition, executive attention, working memory accuracy, sustained attention, and cognitive flexibility (g=0.26–0.64 vs waitlist; g=0.19–0.39 vs active controls) across 111 RCTs.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2023.2248222)

### 2021

1. **The Empirical Status of Mindfulness-Based Interventions: A Systematic Review of 44 Meta-Analyses of RCTs** — *Systematic review*
   - Authors: Goldberg S. B. et al.
   - Effect: Moderate increase
   - Review of 44 meta-analyses of RCTs confirmed MBIs produce consistent, clinically meaningful reductions in anxiety, depression, pain, and stress compared to both passive and active controls across a wide range of conditions.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691620968771)

### 2020

1. **Mindfulness interventions improve momentary and trait measures of attentional control** — *RCT*
   - Authors: Lindsay E. et al.
   - N=153
   - Effect: Moderate increase
   - This pre-registered RCT found that mindfulness training specifically improved present-focused monitoring skills, which in turn drove improvements in momentary and trait attentional control versus an active waitlist control.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000969)

### 2019

1. **Brief Mindfulness Training for Negative Affectivity: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** — *Meta-analysis*
   - Authors: Lindsay E. & Creswell J. D.
   - N=5489
   - Effect: Moderate increase
   - 65 RCTs confirmed brief mindfulness training reliably reduces negative affectivity (depression, anxiety, rumination) with small-to-moderate effect sizes, even in populations without clinical diagnoses.
   - [Link](https://doi.org/10.1037/ccp0000324)
